Parallel Mirror Dual Stripe MR TOF
CAD and Simulation Setup Manual

1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

This manual describes a multi-reflection time-of-flight analyzer with two
parallel electrostatic mirrors, two curved Stripe electrode pairs, two Prisms,
an orthogonal accelerator, and a detector envelope. It provides 53 individual
parts and seven assemblies in native SolidWorks and Parasolid formats. It
explains the component functions, coordinates, ion release, electrical
settings, and observation conditions needed to reproduce the specified
simulation setup.
The specified delivery is a simulation-specific variant with all circular
mounting holes omitted. The hole-free geometry and the native and neutral
file readback have been checked.

The voltage tables and release settings form one coherent simulation setup.
Use the complete set together; do not replace individual voltages with values
from another setup. These settings are not claimed to be globally optimized,
and a complete-source time focus has not been established. The model is not
a fabrication drawing set or a physical performance guarantee.

2. COORDINATES, FILES, AND ASSEMBLIES

All positions and dimensions stated here are in millimeters. Voltages are
relative to the common electrical ground. Kinetic energies are in electron
volts, velocities in millimeters per microsecond, and event times in
microseconds. Integration step limits are stated in nanoseconds.
On CAD import, preserve the units encoded in each file and check the imported
dimensions against the millimeter extents in this manual. Do not manually
apply a factor of 1000 just because the CAD kernel uses SI units internally;
the importer handles the file units.

Assembly axes:
  x: transverse direction; x=0 is the left/right symmetry plane.
  y: slow drift direction along the extended analyzer.
  z: fast acceleration and mirror-reflection direction.

Use the assembly files for world placement. Individual Stripe parts retain
their original native local coordinates. Other individual parts use local
axes corresponding to (project y, project z, project x). A part's local origin
is not necessarily the instrument origin. The complete instance transforms
are recorded in the supplied part-placement and CAD validation records.

The .SLDASM files retain component references and placements. Keep the supplied
parts beside these assemblies. Assembly .x_t files contain flattened bodies
in world coordinates; they do not retain the native component hierarchy.
Bodies at different voltages are not merged. Individual .x_t files retain the
declared part-local coordinates. Parasolid is the qualified neutral format
for these exact curves; unverified STEP files are not substitutes.

3. COMPONENTS AND THEIR FUNCTIONS

Orthogonal accelerator (OA)
The initial beam travels in +y. The accelerator turns it into fast motion in
-z while preserving a much slower y drift. It has two acceleration zones:
the first lies between the repeller at project z=34 mm and the intermediate
grid at z=30 mm; the second extends from that grid to the exit at z=0 mm.
The nominal zone lengths are 4 and 30 mm. The two-zone design aims to bring
ions released at different z positions to a nominal first-order time focus at the
accelerator exit, z=0 mm. Each zone has a linear nominal potential drop and
a nominally uniform acceleration field. Along the -z flight direction, the
first-zone potential decreases from 4749.074377 to 3994.946318 V and the
second-zone potential decreases to 0 V. The nominal field magnitudes are
188.532015 and 133.164877 V/mm, both directed in -z for positive ions.
Real three-dimensional fields near the grids, apertures, and enclosure have
fringe and edge components and are not uniform everywhere. The voltage ratio
compensates part of the starting-position timing difference; the exit focus
is a design purpose, not a claim that the whole release volume has been
verified to arrive simultaneously.
Fifteen ring electrodes grade the second-zone potential. The grounded enclosure
shields the accelerator. Frames support the two modeled grid planes.
The grids are ideal transparent, zero-thickness equipotential sheets in this
model. Ions are not lost through collisions with mesh wires; no actual wire
mesh, transparency loss, or manufacturing detail has been included.

Mirrors
Each mirror has five voltage groups A through E. The two mirrors reverse the
fast z motion repeatedly while the ions drift in y. Their shaped fields are
used to control reflection time and transverse transport. Both mirror ends
use the same A-E voltages. Grounded inner shields limit unwanted field access.
The outer closure plates are electrically connected to the E group, not to
ground. Use the supplied A-E voltage set on both ends. These settings define
the reflection field for this setup; they do not guarantee correction of all
energy, angular, transverse, or higher-order time responses.

Stripes and central ground
S1 and S2 are independently biased pairs of curved electrodes. They guide
the slow drift and its return while the fast mirror reflections continue.
Both electrodes of each pair share one voltage. The central curved part is
grounded. The simulation-specific CAD variant retains the original exact
native B-spline curves, beam slots, and end geometry, but omits all circular
mounting holes. No circular mounting hole may penetrate a beam-slot wall.
The curves are not refitted or replaced by polygons. Field simulations evaluate the same design
with a discretized boundary and omit non-beam mechanical details.

Prisms and shields
P1 and P2 steer injection and returning trajectories. Each Prism consists of
two x-side electrodes at the same voltage and a separate grounded shield.
P1/P2 labels are steering stages, not the two acceleration zones. The P1
grounded shield keeps its existing cross-section and is extended toward the
accelerator outlet, leaving a nominal 1 mm clearance. Its modeled upper end
is z=-1.5 mm next to the outlet frame ending at z=-0.5 mm. This reduces access
of surrounding fields to the connecting beam region; the open beam aperture
still admits edge fields. Neither zero field everywhere nor complete numerical
convergence follows just from adding a grounded shield.

Detector
The detector part is a declared receiving envelope at the returning beam end,
not a detailed MCP detector. Its world extent is x=-25..25, y=-87..-37,
z=95..97 mm. The active return observation plane is z=97 mm for motion in -z.
The model does not supply internal channels, gain, timing electronics, or
detector-response broadening.

C0 reference
The six reference sheets define a candidate grounded enclosing boundary:
x=-162.5..162.5, y=-242..558, z=-425..425 mm. These sheets describe an electrical
boundary assumption, not a manufactured vacuum vessel. Wall thickness, ports,
mounts, and feedthroughs are not specified. In a half-domain simulation x=0
is a symmetry boundary, never an additional grounded wall.

5. ELECTRICAL REFERENCE SETTINGS

Solve an electrostatic field in vacuum with no space-charge coupling. Hold
all listed electrode and grounded-boundary potentials constant throughout
the ion flight. The source release time does not trigger voltage switching.
The provided voltages are simulation reference inputs, not a guarantee of
final bunch focusing or acceptance.

Displayed voltages are rounded to six decimals for readability. Full-precision
values and part-to-electrode assignments remain in the supplied parameter data
and bom.csv. Use those data, not rounded prose, to reproduce a simulation.

6. ION RELEASE AND PATH

Release center in assembly coordinates:
  [x, y, z] = [0, -56.05182899425077, 32] mm.
Release volume:
  Uniform cylinder, axis y, radius 0.5 mm, height 1.0 mm.
  x/z offsets lie inside a radius-0.5 mm disk; they are not independent boxes.
  y extends from -56.55182899425077 to -55.55182899425077 mm.
Species:
  Mass 524 atomic mass units; charge state +1.
Initial motion:
  Direction +y, with zero nominal x/z velocity and zero angular full width.
  Mean slow-direction kinetic energy Ey = 5.374387143093758 eV.
  Ey is kinetic energy, not the y component of the electric field.
  Uniform energy full width 0.1 eV, i.e. mean +/-0.05 eV.
  This is a uniform kinetic-energy distribution, not a Gaussian distribution.
  The equivalent standard deviation of this uniform distribution is
  approximately 0.028868 eV.
Birth time:
  All ions start at t=0; no source-time spread is included.

The accelerator geometric axis is at assembly y=-55 mm; the release center
has an additional y offset of -1.051828994250772 mm. The net accelerator
geometry placement [0, -55, -6] mm is applied once. Its exit reference is at
project z=0. The release position above is already in final assembly coordinates
and must not receive that placement a second time.

The intended path is OA -> P1 -> negative mirror pre-reflection -> P2 ->
mirror and Stripe propagation -> natural P2 return -> positive mirror turn
-> detector. Positive ions leave the accelerator in -z. At the release
center, the nominal accelerator potential implies an exit z kinetic energy
of 4372.010347796059 eV for charge +1; this is not the release Ey value.

K and phase counting
The design ratio K is the time for one slow y excursion from the entry region
to its drift turning point and back, divided by one complete fast oscillation
between the two mirrors. K=24.5 corresponds to 24 complete fast periods plus
one half-period, or 49 half-periods. It does not mean 24.5 individual reflections.
For phase observation, set count zero at the first positive-z mirror turn
after P2 with positive y motion. Each subsequent physical mirror turn adds
one half-period. The target is the opposite-mirror turn at count 49. Earlier
injection reflections and later return reflections are recorded separately;
their inclusion in a raw total must not change this main-excursion definition.
The nominal first positive-mirror turn is at y=0 mm, and the nominal slow
turn is at y=340 mm. These are matching targets, not forced trajectory resets.

Nominal injection angle and reference state
The nominal angle is 2.008022871136829 degrees measured in the y-z plane from
the positive z direction toward positive y. For positive-z travel its tangent
is vy/vz=0.035060966450536406. It is a theoretical matching value associated
with the supplied mirror/Stripe setup, not an independent initial angle to
apply to ions released inside the accelerator.
Measure the actual angle on the first positive-z crossing of the P2 exit
reference plane z=33 mm. This lies in the selected reference interval
z=26..40 mm; it is not assumed to be a perfectly field-free plane.
One center-ray reference at this plane is:
  Position [x, y, z] = [0, -9.20013672284, 33] mm.
  Velocity [vx, vy, vz] = [0, 1.40252949556, 40.1253546944] mm/microsecond.
  Actual angle atan(vy/vz) = 2.0018843151693746 degrees.
This is an observed finite-field state for comparison, not a replacement
release condition. The angle can vary along a trajectory; do not force the
nominal angle at every plane or inside a mirror turning field.

7. OBSERVATIONS TERMINATION AND MODEL LIMITS

The geometry and voltages are symmetric in x. An ideal center ion released
with x=0 and vx=0 stays in that symmetry plane. Left/right paired trajectories
can be mirror images while both arrive later or earlier than the center;
symmetry alone does not remove transverse even-time response or all aberrations.

Use one clock starting at the common release time t=0. Record crossings of
the accelerator geometric exit z=0, the P2 exit reference z=33, central z=0
planes during reflection, mirror turns, the slow y turn, and the final
detector surface separately. The accelerator exit and an outer numerical
domain exit are different observations. Crossing z=0 elsewhere in the
instrument is not evidence of accelerator time focusing.

A mirror turn is an outward-to-inward reversal of vz in the appropriate
mirror region. A slow turn is a positive-to-negative reversal of vy during
the main drift excursion. Neither event stops the ion or changes a voltage.
The target count 49 is a phase observation, not a programmed voltage switch.
Locate plane crossings and reversal events between trajectory samples;
nearest output times alone are insufficient for accurate timing.

A detector hit requires the valid return sequence, travel in -z, and contact
with the z=97 mm receiving plane inside x=-25..25 and y=-87..-37 mm. Earlier
crossings of the same plane during injection are only observations. Collision
with any other solid conductor terminates the ray as a loss. Preserve the raw
collision position and time; a valid detector contact can be localized to the
receiving plane, but a wall collision must not be relabeled as a hit. Re-entry
into the accelerator after initial extraction is an invalid return path.
Leaving available field support or obtaining a nonfinite field is a numerical
failure, not a physical collision. The ideal transparent OA sheets are field
boundaries rather than absorbing wire surfaces; the surrounding frames and
enclosure remain solid collision objects.

For SIMION tracking, the stated reference uses trajectory quality Q=8 and a
maximum integration step of 4 ns. Field resolution, event localization, and
collision-surface resolution also affect the result. Check the quantities
needed for the intended simulation rather than assuming that a small step
alone proves accuracy. A center-ray detector hit does not establish a time
focus for the full release volume. Retain all lost ions when evaluating a
source distribution.

Space charge, realistic angular spread, source birth-time spread, detector
timing response, manufacturing errors, and actual vacuum-vessel ports are not
covered by this delivery. The model is an engineering simulation reference,
not a final physical performance guarantee or fabrication drawing set.
